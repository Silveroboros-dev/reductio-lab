## Origin and reliability

All eight exact market tickers appear on indexed Kalshi pages, and those pages broadly agree with the supplied primary resolution rules. This verifies that markets with those identifiers have been published; it **does not prove** that the JSON was directly returned by the API, verify its `status`, prices, volumes or capture time, or establish that all fields are current and complete. The file itself has no HTTP request, response headers, source receipt or retrieval timestamp; `updated_time` is market metadata, not an observation timestamp. Kalshi documents `min_updated_ts` as tracking non-trading changes, so an old `updated_time` alone does not establish stale quotes. [Kalshi Get Markets](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/market/get-markets).

The Fed `rules_secondary` contains an inconsistent example: “Hike   25 bps” resolves Yes and “Hike 25 bps” resolves No for the same 50-bp hike, whereas [Kalshi's page](https://kalshi.com/markets/kxfeddecision/fed-meeting) displays `>25` for the Yes side. Other independent sites reporting Kalshi data also omit the `>` character, e.g. [predictionsmarket.com](https://predictionsmarket.com/markets/economics/fed-decision-in-dec-2026). Therefore the issue is real but its origin is unknown: an upstream API, processing, or display bug is also possible. Do not silently repair original bytes. Bind any separately observed corrected rule with its own provenance and use the inconsistent fragment as a declared conflict until adjudicated.

Three rows carry `expected_expiration_time` later than `latest_expiration_time`: the Anthropic government contract (June versus January 2027), Claude 6 (December versus January 2027), and recursive self-improvement (2029 versus January 2027). These are suspicious field-level inconsistencies that merit a direct-source check; they do not, by themselves, establish fabrication or tell us which field controls settlement. Distinguish trading close, expected resolution and latest expiration, and rely on separately verified contractual wording.

Search index copies, sometimes crawled last month, show different changing prices from this file. Such differences are expected for dynamic markets and are not by themselves evidence of fabrication. `cursor: ""`, eight events and alphabetic field order likewise do not prove anything: Kalshi [supports a comma-separated ticker filter](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/market/get-markets), and formatting may change during saving or transport.

## The central experimental limitation

When the human thesis and complete contract rules are already fixed, a search result about recent events should not ordinarily change their semantic fit. The most informative design prespecifies for each case what missing, genuinely time-sensitive evidence could change an answer or warranted abstention, alongside controls where a query is unnecessary. Source data should be used only when the returned observation actually bears on that prespecified gap; do not retroactively invent an expectation. A fresh result might help event state, source/version status or evidence sufficiency, but it cannot be treated as an instruction to rewrite the thesis or rules. Scoring C versus B requires the same model, usable data and blind evaluation of the same outcomes; source acquisition cost and reasoning cost stay separate.
